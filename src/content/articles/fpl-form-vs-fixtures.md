Every FPL transfer comes down to one argument: form against fixtures. The player in form has just scored, so you want him. The player with kind fixtures has easy games coming, so you want him too. You cannot buy everyone, so you need a way to choose. The framework below is simple, and it works for almost every transfer decision you will make this season.

## Form tells you what happened

Form is a record of the past. A player who has scored in three straight games is in form, but form is also the noisiest thing in FPL. A striker can score twice from two shots and look unstoppable, then go four games without a return. That does not mean form is useless. A player in form is usually confident, usually starting, and usually getting chances. But form alone is a bad reason to buy. Ask what is behind the returns. Is he getting shots every game, or did he score from his only touch? Is he taking set pieces, or was it a one-off penalty? The underlying numbers matter more than the points total.

## Fixtures tell you what is coming

Fixtures are a forecast, and forecasts are more useful than history when you are buying for the future. A good run of fixtures means more chances, more goals, and more clean sheets. The classic way to judge fixtures is by the opponent's strength, home or away, and how many of the next few games are kind. A player with four good fixtures in a row is a better buy than a player with one good fixture and three bad ones, even if the second player is in better form today. Fixtures are also why timing matters: buy a player the week before his good run starts, not the week it ends.

## The order that works

Judge fixtures first, then form. First, find the teams with the best next three or four fixtures. Then, inside those teams, find the players who are actually returning points or getting chances. That order stops you from buying a player in form whose fixtures are about to turn nasty, and it stops you from buying a player with great fixtures who never shoots. When both line up, a good player in form with kind fixtures ahead, that is the transfer to make, even if it costs a little more.

## The exceptions

There are two exceptions. The first is the elite premium player. The very best attackers can score against anyone, so their fixtures matter less. You do not sell a premium because he has two hard games. The second is the enabler, the cheap player you buy to free up money elsewhere. For him, fixtures barely matter. He just needs to play and cost as little as possible. Everything in between should pass the fixtures-first test.

## A quick checklist before you confirm

Before you hit confirm on any transfer, ask four questions. Does his team have good fixtures for the next three gameweeks? Is he starting and playing most minutes? Is he getting chances, not just points? And does the transfer improve your team for the next month, not just the next game? If the answer is yes to all four, make the transfer. If it is no to two or more, keep your transfer and wait. The best FPL managers are not the ones who make the most transfers. They are the ones who make the right ones.
