Expected goals, written xG, and expected assists, written xA, are the two numbers that separate serious FPL managers from everyone else. They look technical, but the idea is simple: they measure the quality of the chances a player gets, not just whether the ball went in. If you understand them, you can spot the next points scorer before the crowd does. If you ignore them, you will keep buying players who just got lucky.

## What expected goals actually means

Every shot has a probability of becoming a goal, based on where it was taken from, what type of shot it was, and the situation around it. A shot from six yards out in the middle of the goal might be worth 0.7 expected goals, which means it becomes a goal about seven times out of ten. A shot from thirty yards might be worth 0.03. Add up all of a player's shots and you get his xG, the number of goals he should have scored given the chances he had. A player with an xG of 4.5 who has scored two goals has been unlucky, or wasteful. A player with an xG of 1.2 who has scored four has been lucky, and the goals will probably stop.

## What expected assists means

Expected assists works the same way for the pass before the shot. Every pass that leads to a shot gets a value based on how good the chance was. A through ball that puts a striker clean through might be worth 0.5 expected assists. A sideways pass that leads to a shot from distance might be worth 0.02. Add them up and you get xA, the number of assists a player should have. Creative midfielders and full-backs who cross well tend to have high xA, and like xG, it is a much better guide to future returns than the assist count alone.

## How to use them for transfers

The trick is to compare the expected numbers with the actual returns. A player whose xG is much higher than his goals is due a return, and he is often cheap because everyone else has sold him. A player whose goals are much higher than his xG is due a blank, and he is often expensive because everyone is buying him. Look at the numbers over the last four to six games, not one game, because one game is too small a sample. And look at xG per game, not just the total, so you can compare players who have played different minutes.

## What the numbers cannot tell you

Expected goals cannot tell you everything. They do not measure finishing skill, so a great finisher will beat his xG over a season and a poor finisher will fall short. They do not measure whether a player will keep his place in the team, or whether his fixtures are about to get harder. And they are useless in tiny samples: one game tells you almost nothing. Use xG and xA as a filter, not a verdict. They tell you which players are getting chances. Your job is to decide which of those players will keep getting them.

## A simple routine

Once a week, look at the xG and xA tables for the last four gameweeks. Write down the players at the top who are not already in your team. Then check three things: do they start every game, do their fixtures stay kind for the next few weeks, and can you afford them? If the answer is yes to all three, that is your transfer. Do that every week and you will buy players before they haul, instead of after.
