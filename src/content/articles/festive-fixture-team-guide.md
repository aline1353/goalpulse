December is the hardest month in Fantasy Premier League. Teams play four or five games in about two weeks, managers rotate their squads to cope, and players who were nailed-on starters suddenly sit on the bench. The managers who plan for it gain ground. The ones who do not spend the month chasing fires. Here is how to build a team that survives the festive rush.

## Why December is different

The problem is not the number of games. It is the gaps between them. Two or three days between matches is not enough to recover, so managers rest players even when they are fit. That is rotation, and it hits every position. A striker who plays every game in November might start two of four in December. A full-back might be rested for the easiest fixture of the lot. If your team depends on eleven specific players, December will hurt you. If your team has depth, it will not.

## Build your bench first

The festive period is the one time of the season when your bench genuinely matters every week. A strong bench turns a rotation headache into a free substitution: your benched starter sits, your substitute plays, and you lose nothing. That means a playing goalkeeper as your second keeper, defenders who start for their clubs, and a midfielder or forward who gets regular minutes. It costs a little more than a dead bench, but in December it pays for itself twice over.

## Pick durable players

Some players are more rotation-proof than others. Goalkeepers almost never rotate. Centre-backs rotate less than full-backs. Penalty takers and set-piece takers keep their value even when their minutes drop, because they can return in twenty minutes. And players in teams with thin squads often have to play every game, because there is nobody to replace them. When you are choosing between two similar players before December, take the durable one.

## Plan your transfers around the congestion

Do not waste transfers in December fixing one-week problems. A player rested for one game is not a problem, he is a rotation. Save your transfers for real issues: injuries, suspensions, and players who have genuinely lost their place. And keep one transfer banked if you can, so you can react when team news drops. The managers who do best in December are the ones who make the fewest panicked moves.

## What to do right now

You do not need to rebuild your team today. But start the work in November. Make sure your second goalkeeper plays. Make sure at least two of your bench players start most weeks. And when you make a transfer, ask whether the player you are buying is likely to play four games in two weeks, or two. If the answer is two, buy someone else. December rewards depth, patience, and durable players. Build for that, and the festive rush becomes the month you climb.
