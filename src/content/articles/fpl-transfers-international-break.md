The international break gives FPL managers two weeks between deadlines, and that is a trap. With no gameweek to prepare for, it is tempting to make transfers on day one, chase price rises, and tinker your team into a mess. The managers who gain ground during a break are the ones who wait. Here is how to use the pause properly.

## Do not rush your transfers

Your free transfer does not expire, and you get another one when the next deadline comes. Making a transfer on the first day of the break means locking in a decision before you know who is fit. A player you bring in could pick up an injury on international duty. A player you sell could come back in great form. Wait until the final days before the Gameweek 11 deadline, when you have the most information, and then decide.

## Watch the internationals, not the noise

The thing that matters during the break is who plays, who travels far, and who comes back hurt. A starter who plays two full games for his country is more of a fatigue risk than a player who stayed at home. Watch for injury reports in the second week of the break, and pay attention to the Friday press conferences before the deadline. Managers will usually say if a returning international is a doubt, and that is the information your transfers should be based on.

## Price changes still happen

Prices still move during the break, because transfers in and out still count. A player lots of managers are buying will rise, and one lots of managers are selling will fall. That tempts people to move early to catch a rise, but a 0.1 rise is worth less than a fit player. If you are selling a player who is falling, do it early. If you are buying, the rise is rarely worth the injury risk of deciding two weeks early. Patience is usually worth more than 0.1 in team value.

## Plan for Gameweek 11, not Gameweek 10

The break means your next deadline is Gameweek 11 on 21 November, not the round that just finished. Judge your transfers on the Gameweek 11 fixtures: Manchester City at home to Fulham, Chelsea at home to Leeds, Tottenham at home to Ipswich, and Arsenal away at Newcastle. If a player has a bad fixture in Gameweek 11 but a great run after it, he can still be worth buying. Look at the next three or four fixtures, not just the next one.

## Use the time to fix your structure

A break is the best time to fix a structural problem in your team, because you can think instead of reacting. Is your bench too weak to cover a rotation risk? Is your money tied up in a player who never plays? Do you have too much value in defence and not enough in attack? Sort those things out with a calm head, make at most one or two changes, and go into Gameweek 11 with a team you actually believe in.
