The Premier League returns on Saturday 21 November after a 13-day international break, and Gameweek 11 wastes no time. Manchester City host Fulham in the early kick-off, Newcastle welcome leaders Arsenal in the evening game, and Sunday brings Liverpool against Manchester United at Anfield. It is the first round back after the pause, and it could reshape the top of the table.

## The table going into the break

Arsenal lead the way on 24 points from nine matches, three clear of Manchester City on 21. Brighton sit third on 19 points, Chelsea fourth on 16, and Liverpool fifth on 15. Those are the standings after nine rounds, with Gameweek 10 still to be played on 6 to 8 November before the break begins. Whatever happens in that round, the chasing pack will see Gameweek 11 as the first real chance to close the gap after the pause.

## Saturday's games

The round opens at 12:30 with Manchester City against Fulham at the Etihad. City will expect to win at home, but Fulham have made a habit of being awkward opponents, and the champions will want to keep the pressure on Arsenal before the evening game. The 15:00 games see Bournemouth host Nottingham Forest, Aston Villa take on Sunderland, Chelsea welcome Leeds United, Coventry City face Crystal Palace, and Tottenham host Ipswich Town. The day ends at 17:30 with Newcastle United against Arsenal at St James' Park, the hardest test of the leaders' season so far on paper.

## Sunday and Monday

Sunday has two games: Hull City against Brighton at 14:00, and then the big one, Liverpool against Manchester United at 16:30. Liverpool sit fifth and need the win to stay in touch with the top four, while United, down in 17th, would love nothing more than a result at Anfield to kick-start their season. The round closes on Monday night when Brentford host Everton at 20:00.

## What to watch

The title race is the obvious thread. If Arsenal win at Newcastle, their lead grows and the break will feel like it came at the wrong time for everyone else. If they slip, City can close the gap to nothing with a win over Fulham. Below them, Brighton travel to Hull with third place to protect, and Chelsea's home game against Leeds is the kind of fixture a top-four side has to win. For FPL managers, the break means two weeks to plan transfers, and Gameweek 11 is the first deadline that matters after it.
