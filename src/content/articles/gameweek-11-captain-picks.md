The captain gets double points, so the armband is the single biggest decision of any FPL gameweek. Get it right and you can gain twenty points on your rivals in one weekend. Get it wrong and you spend the week watching someone else's captain haul. Gameweek 11, the first round back after the international break, is a good week to think carefully about it.

## Start with the fixture, not the name

The best captaincy rule is simple: pick a player from a team with a good fixture. A great player in a terrible fixture is a worse captain than a good player in a great one. In Gameweek 11, Manchester City host Fulham at home, Chelsea host Leeds at Stamford Bridge, and Tottenham host Ipswich. Those are the three standout home fixtures of the round, and the attacking players in those teams should be at the top of your shortlist.

## The safe pick

The safe captain is the most-owned premium attacker with the best fixture. Most managers will land in the same place, and that is fine. A safe captain protects your rank: if he hauls, you haul with everyone else, and if he blanks, you blank with everyone else. When you are happy with your overall rank and the fixture is strong, the safe pick is usually the right one. There is no prize for being different for the sake of it.

## The differential captain

A differential captain is a lower-owned player you back to outscore the popular choice. It makes sense in two situations: when you need to make up ground in your mini-league, or when the popular captain's fixture is not actually that good. Gameweek 11 has candidates beyond the obvious names. Arsenal travel to Newcastle, which is a hard game, so the popular Arsenal attackers are less appealing than usual. Liverpool host Manchester United, and a Liverpool attacker at home against a struggling United side is exactly the kind of pick that can win a week if it lands.

## What the break changes

The international break adds one extra wrinkle. Players returning from international duty can be tired, or they can pick up injuries, so wait for team news before you lock in your captain. A player who played two full internationals on the other side of the world is a riskier captain than one who stayed at home and trained. Check the press conferences on Friday before the deadline, and have a back-up captain in mind in case your first choice is a doubt.

## A simple way to decide

Ask three questions. Does he play for a team with a good fixture? Is he likely to play the full ninety minutes? And is he the main goal threat in his team? If the answer is yes to all three, he is a captain. If you have two players who pass the test, take the one with the better fixture. And if you are chasing in your mini-league, take the less-owned one. The armband rewards boldness, but only when the fixture backs it up.
