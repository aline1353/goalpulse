Your overall rank is a number. Your mini-league is personal. It is your friends, your family, your colleagues, and the bragging rights that last all year. Most FPL managers care far more about beating the people they know than about their overall rank, and the strategy for winning a mini-league is different from the strategy for climbing the overall leaderboard. Here is how it works.

## How mini-leagues work

A mini-league is a private group of FPL managers. You join with a code, or someone invites you, and your total points are ranked against everyone else in the group. The league table updates every gameweek, and the winner is the manager with the most points at the end of the season. There is no head-to-head unless your league is set up that way: in a classic mini-league, every point counts the same, and the table never resets. That means consistency beats the occasional big week.

## Know your rivals, not just your team

The biggest mistake in mini-league play is ignoring what your rivals own. If the manager above you owns the same eleven players as you, you cannot catch him, because you will score the same points every week. To gain ground, you need to be different where it matters. Look at the most-owned players in your league, not the whole game. If everyone in your league has the same captain, captaining someone else is the only way to move. If nobody has a player with great fixtures, he is your edge. Mini-leagues are won by owning the right different players, not by owning the best players.

## When to play it safe, and when to gamble

Your position decides your strategy. If you are top of your mini-league, play safe. Copy the template, captain the popular pick, and let your rivals take the risks. Your lead is worth more than a gamble. If you are chasing, you have to be different. Take the differential captain, bring in the low-owned player with kind fixtures, and use your chips at a different time from your rivals. The gap only closes if you do something they are not doing. And if you are miles behind with a few weeks left, go all in: there is nothing to lose, and a big differential week is the only thing that can save you.

## Chips are weapons in a mini-league

Chips matter more in mini-leagues than anywhere else, because timing them differently from your rivals is a free advantage. If everyone in your league plays their Wildcard in the same week, nobody gains. If you hold yours for a double gameweek while they burn theirs early, you gain. The same goes for Bench Boost and Triple Captain. Watch when your rivals use their chips, and try to use yours in a different, better week. And never waste a chip just because everyone else is using one.

## The long game

Mini-leagues are won in the second half of the season, not the first. Early on, just stay in touch: do not fall fifty points behind by Christmas, do not take hits every week, and do not panic. Keep your transfers for real problems, keep your chips for the double gameweeks, and keep an eye on your rivals' teams. Then, when the run-in comes, be the manager who is different in the right places. That is how mini-leagues are won.
