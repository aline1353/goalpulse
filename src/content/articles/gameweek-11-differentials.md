A differential is a player owned by only a small percentage of managers. When he scores, almost nobody else gets those points, so your rank jumps. When he blanks, you lose almost nothing, because almost nobody else had him either. That is the whole appeal. Gameweek 11, with its mix of strong home fixtures and a big game at Anfield, is a good week to think about one.

## What makes a good differential

A good differential is not just a random cheap player. He needs three things: a good fixture, a real role in his team, and a price that lets you afford him without wrecking your structure. Look for attackers who take set pieces, penalty takers in teams with kind fixtures, and midfielders who play high up the pitch for teams at home. Avoid players who are only in the team because someone else is injured, unless that injury is long-term. A one-week stand-in is a trap.

## Where to look in Gameweek 11

Start with the home fixtures. Chelsea host Leeds, Tottenham host Ipswich, and Manchester City host Fulham. The most-owned players in those teams will be in millions of sides, so the differential value is in the second and third attackers: the winger who is not the obvious pick, the midfielder who takes corners, the full-back who gets forward. Bournemouth host Nottingham Forest at home, and Aston Villa host Sunderland. Both are the kind of games where a home attacker at a low ownership can return. On Sunday, Hull host Brighton, and a Brighton attacker away at a newly promoted side is another route to difference.

## The risk, stated plainly

Differentials fail more often than template players. That is why they are differentials. The mistake is not picking one, it is building your whole week around one. A differential should be one transfer, maybe two, in an otherwise solid team. If he hauls, you climb. If he blanks, your template players still carry your score. Never captain a differential unless you are chasing hard in your mini-league and need a swing, and never take a points hit to bring one in.

## How to use one properly

The best time to own a differential is when you have a reason beyond hope. Maybe his fixtures turn kind for the next three or four weeks, not just one. Maybe he has just nailed down a starting spot. Maybe he takes penalties and his team is at home to a side that gives them away. If you can explain the pick in one sentence that is about football and not about ownership, it is a good differential. If the only reason is that nobody else owns him, it is a lottery ticket, and lottery tickets do not win mini-leagues.
