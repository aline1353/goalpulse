If you do not understand the scoring system, you cannot pick a good team. FPL rewards far more than goals, and some of its most valuable points come from places new managers never look. Here is the system in plain English.

## Playing time and goals

Any player who takes the pitch earns points for appearing, with a little more for playing most of the match. Goals are worth more for defenders and goalkeepers than for midfielders, and more for midfielders than for forwards - the game deliberately rewards the rarer event. That is why a goal-scoring defender can outscore a striker over a season.

## Assists are broader than you think

In FPL, an assist is not only the final pass. Deflections, rebounds and even winning a penalty or a free kick that is then scored can count, depending on the game official definitions. The lesson is simple: players involved in attacking moves score well even when they are not the one finishing them.

## Clean sheets and conceding

Goalkeepers and defenders earn clean sheet points when their team concedes nothing while they are on the pitch. Midfielders earn a smaller clean sheet reward. But defenders and goalkeepers also lose points for goals conceded, which is why a defender in a leaky defence can be a trap even if he attacks well.

## Bonus points: the hidden decider

After every match, the three best-performing players receive bonus points, decided by a statistics-based system that weighs actions like chances created, tackles, recoveries and saves. Bonus points regularly decide close gameweeks, and they are a big reason why all-round midfielders and attacking full-backs are so popular.

## Cards and misses

Yellow and red cards cost points, and a missed penalty costs points too. A saved penalty, on the other hand, is a major reward for a goalkeeper. The overall message is consistent: FPL rewards players who are involved in everything their team does well.
