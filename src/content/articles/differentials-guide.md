A differential is a player owned by only a small share of managers. If he scores big and your rivals do not own him, you climb past them all at once. That is the appeal - and the trap. Differentials are a tool, not a strategy on their own.

## Why differentials matter in mini-leagues

In the overall global ranking, steady template picks win. In a mini-league against friends, the maths is different: you are not trying to beat ten million people, you are trying to beat twenty. One well-chosen differential returning in a week when the popular players blank can move you several places in a single gameweek.

## What makes a good differential

A good differential is not just an unpopular player. He needs three things: a kind fixture or two, a role that brings attacking returns (set pieces, penalties, advanced positioning), and a realistic chance of starting. An unpopular player who sits on the bench is not a differential, he is a wasted slot.

## When NOT to chase differentials

Early in the season, and when you are already leading your mini-league, differentials are usually wrong. The template exists because those players are the best value. Differentials earn their place when you are chasing, when the template is failing, or when a player fixtures turn and the crowd has not noticed yet.

## The one-differential rule

A useful discipline: change one differential at a time. A team full of punts is just a lottery ticket. One or two well-researched differentials inside an otherwise solid team gives you upside without destroying your floor. If the punt fails, your team still scores. If it lands, you fly.
