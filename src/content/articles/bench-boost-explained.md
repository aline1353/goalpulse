The Bench Boost is one of the four chips in Fantasy Premier League, and it is the one most managers waste. It sounds simple: for one gameweek, your four substitutes score points too. But most benches are full of cheap players who never play, so the chip returns four points and a shrug. Played properly, with a strong bench and the right gameweek, it can be worth twenty or thirty points. That is a bigger swing than most captain decisions.

## How the chip works

When you play Bench Boost, all fifteen players in your squad score in that gameweek, not just the eleven you start. Your goalkeeper, defenders, midfielders and forwards on the bench all count. The chip can only be used once per season, and it cannot be combined with any other chip in the same gameweek. You activate it when you confirm your team for that gameweek, and the extra points are added at the end of the round. There is no way to undo it once the deadline passes.

## Why most Bench Boosts fail

The usual mistake is playing it with a normal bench. A normal FPL bench has a 4.0 goalkeeper who never plays, two cheap defenders who might get two points each, and a 4.5 midfielder who gets a cameo. That bench scores about four to eight points, which is barely better than doing nothing. The chip is not the problem. The bench is. A Bench Boost is only worth playing when your bench is strong enough to score on its own, and building that bench takes planning.

## How to prepare your bench

Preparation starts two or three gameweeks before you plan to use the chip. You want four bench players who all start for their clubs and all have decent fixtures in the target week. That usually means spending a little more on your bench than usual: a playing 4.5 goalkeeper instead of a 4.0 non-player, defenders from teams with kind fixtures, and a midfielder or forward who actually gets minutes. You do not need stars on the bench. You need starters with kind fixtures. A bench of four starters in good games can score fifteen to twenty-five points.

## When to play it

The best Bench Boost gameweeks are the ones where the most teams play twice, because your bench players get two games instead of one. Those double gameweeks usually come later in the season, when postponed fixtures pile up. A double gameweek Bench Boost with a strong bench is the highest-ceiling chip play in the game. If no double gameweek suits your team, the next best option is a normal gameweek where all four of your bench players have good home fixtures. Never play it in a gameweek where one of your bench players does not start. That is points thrown away.

## The plan, in short

Pick your target gameweek early, build your bench towards it over two or three weeks, and only press the button when all four bench players start and have decent fixtures. If your bench is not ready, wait. The chip does not expire until the end of the season, and a Bench Boost played late with a strong bench is worth far more than one played early with a weak one.
